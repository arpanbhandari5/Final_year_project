# User-facing interpretation (research copy; not wired into production)

This analysis classifies task-level exposure under the published GPTs-are-GPTs E0/E1/E2 taxonomy.

It describes contextual task exposure. It is not a prediction of personal job loss, unemployment, employment probability, or worker replacement.

## Occupation

**Contextual task exposure**

This occupation contains tasks represented across the E0, E1, and E2 categories in the published GPTs-are-GPTs taxonomy.

These categories describe task-level exposure context and should not be interpreted as a prediction of what will happen to an individual worker.

If the occupation is in the 923-occupation benchmark but not in the 800 common-job subset: research/model coverage may be available; product common-occupation coverage is outside the selected 800.

If the occupation is in neither set: do not fabricate an exposure classification; communicate insufficient benchmark coverage.

## Resume

**Task overlap**

Your resume contains evidence related to tasks represented in different exposure categories.

This describes task context and overlap with the benchmark taxonomy. It does not estimate your personal probability of job loss.

## Model confidence

Use: “Model confidence for this task classification.”

Do not use: confidence that the worker will lose their job; employment confidence; calibrated probability (unless a calibration study exists).

## Explanations

Label coefficient/n-gram lists as **model-derived textual associations**.

Do not claim that a word causes automation, that a task causes job loss, or that a skill prevents unemployment.

## Prohibited model-generated conclusions

- safe job / unsafe job
- job-loss risk / replacement probability / chance of unemployment
- AI-proof job
